Reviewer: Codex, working from the committed repository and captures.
Requested by: this project, deliberately adversarially — the prompt asked for the
flaw, not for confirmation, and listed eight suspected weaknesses up front.

**Verdict: reject D-24 as written.** Accepted. D-24 is marked REJECTED in

Every load-bearing figure below was independently re-verified against

## 1. What was wrong with the analysis

### (a) No deduplication — a violation of our own Rule 8

`MEASUREMENT_METHOD.md` Rule 8 reads: *"Deduplicate by measurement identity
before computing statistics… Group by `gpu_frame` before averaging."*

The E-51 analysis did not. **350 of 7028 dwell rows repeat a `gpu_frame`** — 5.0%
of the input was stale readings counted as fresh measurements. The same contract
is stated in `GovernorSample`'s header comment in our own source.

This is not an oversight in unfamiliar territory. It is a written rule, in this
repository, that the analysis ignored.

### (b) The serpentine turnaround was pooled

The sweep runs out and back, so the **endpoint preset is visited twice
consecutively**. Segmenting by contiguous runs of `preset_public` merges both
NativeAA dwells into a single 17-second block (t=128–145 s against an 8 s dwell)
and attributes all of it to pass 1.

The headline "sweep 1 versus sweep 2" comparison was therefore a **seven-point
fit against a six-point fit**. The serpentine traversal is a documented property
of the cycler — it is why the sweep count must be even — and was known when the
analysis was written.

### (c) "Ten minutes apart" was wrong

Pass 1 runs t=70–125 s, pass 2 runs t=146–198 s: **90–120 seconds apart**, not
ten minutes. The two samples are far less independent than the claim of scene
separation required.

## 2. What the corrected numbers say

| quantity | as published in E-51 | corrected |
|---|---|---|
| fit | `8.22 + 12.34·f` (mean) | `9.26 + 15.11·f` (P95, dedup) |
| worst residual | 0.39 ms | **0.90 ms** (margin is 1.00 ms) |
| fixed/scaled shift between passes | 43% | **115%** (0.300 → 0.646) |
| `UltraPerformance → Performance` | 1.167 (undedup mean) | **1.290** (dedup pooled P95) |
| shipped seed | "too pessimistic" | 1.243 — mildly **optimistic** |

Two consequences kill the proposal on its own terms:

**It fails its own stated currency.** D-24 said the fit "must be on P95, not the
mean" — and then argued entirely from means. On P95 the worst residual is 0.90 ms
against a 1.00 ms landing margin: the model's own error nearly fills the safety
margin it is meant to predict into.

**It does not do what it was proposed to do.** The motivating case was a climb
refused at a live P95 of 11.25 ms. The fitted curve predicts a ratio of 1.192 and
a landing of **13.41 ms** against the 12.89 ms limit — so **D-24 refuses the same
climb**. The premise that a better cost model would release it was wrong.

### Held-out performance is a wash

Across 13 actual applied climbs, comparing predicted landing against realised P95
three seconds after application (reviewer's figures):

| | MAE |
|---|---|
| D-18, as logged | 0.774 ms |
| D-24 scaled fit | 0.764 ms — wins 5 of 13 |
| **D-14 additive form** | **0.680 ms** |

A 0.010 ms difference is noise. And the additive form — which D-24 discarded
without testing, on a question D-14 explicitly records as unresolved — beats
both.

Held-out sweep prediction across three captures favours the fit by 14%, 18% and
42%; but **at the first three rungs, the controller's actual operating region, the
model loses in two of three captures** (0.413 vs 0.402 ms, 0.628 vs 0.439 ms).

## 3. The eight pre-registered concerns, judged

| # | concern | judgment |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | mean vs P95 | **Real and decisive.** Residual 0.41 → 0.90 ms. |
| 2 | pairwise vs global | **D-24 historically correct.** E-27 says "implied `k` per adjacent step"; slope noise scales as ~1/Δf, and converting to `k=B/A` worsens conditioning. But correcting E-27 does not validate D-24. |
| 3 | one session | **Real.** Earlier captures cut the claimed advantage sharply. |
| 4 | re-anchoring | **Real and decisive.** Comparing fitted against raw quantities mechanically favours the fit. Held-out landing error is the fair test, and it is a wash. |
| 5 | lost adaptivity | **Real.** The fitted shape changes substantially even between adjacent passes; D-24 deletes the only mechanism that can change shape later. |
| 6 | `f = scale²` | **Reasonable first-order regressor, not a law.** NVIDIA's API keeps output dimensions explicit while quality mode selects render dimensions — consistent with input-pixel work in `B·f` and output-sized work in `A` — but NVIDIA does not claim runtime is linear in input pixels. |

## 4. The conclusions worth keeping

**This is an exploration problem, not an estimation problem.** A controller cannot
learn the cost of an action it never takes. No passive estimator removes that
fact, which is why swapping estimators was never going to fix the stuck rung. If
a rung is unobserved, the honest answer is an explicit, bounded calibration probe
under its own rule — not exploration smuggled inside an optimistic cost model.

**Correcting a decision's justification is not the same as validating its
replacement.** E-27 really did use an ill-conditioned estimator, and D-18 really
was justified by evidence that did not show what it was taken to show. That
finding stands. It says nothing about whether the proposed replacement is better,
and D-24 treated it as though it did.

**Every error pushed the same way.** The failure was not arithmetic. The analysis
compared fitted quantities against raw ones, scored stability on the data used to
fit, and switched currency to whichever supported the point — because the same
author wrote both the hypothesis and its test. That is the structural reason the
second opinion was worth asking for, and the reason for Rule 11 below.

**Priority.** An 11.0% versus 0.3% missed-frame discrepancy between two presets
matters more than a 0.01 ms improvement in a cost model. E-49 is the important
open problem; this was not it.

## 5. Code scope D-24 missed

For any future attempt, beyond D-24's own deletion list:

- `_presetBeforeChange`, `_lastDecisionP95Gpu`, `_lastDecisionPreset` are also
  calibration-only state in `GovernorCore.h`.
- Public `StepRatio`, `StepMeasured`, `StepObservations` diagnostics and the
  decision reason strings all change.
- `RatioIndex` **must remain** — D-20's failure memory indexes with it.
- Replay result fields, `ObserveStepRatios`, `ReportStepRatios` and their tests
  depend on the table.
- `TraceReplay.cpp` already has a **mean-based** `FitCostModel`. Reusing it, or
  changing it to P95, also changes the replay oracle and confounds the very
  comparison being run.
- The production governor is constructed **before** the sweep, and
  `Reporter::Finish` aggregates frame-time records, not deduplicated GPU dwell
  P95. A new calibration-data path and a fit-injection API are required.
- Missing validation: incomplete sweeps, timed-sample coverage, duplicate
  identity handling, non-positive coefficients or denominators, residual
  thresholds, and logging which statistic was used and which fallback applied.

## 6. Pre-registration required before any retry

D-24's refutation condition was too weak — it would have passed a proposal that
is a wash. Any future cost-model comparison must pre-register:

1. Exact nearest-rank P95 definition, `gpu_frame` deduplication, visit
   segmentation (including the serpentine turnaround), pooling rule, and equal
   weighting per preset.
2. **Held-out sessions and held-out transitions.** Never score stability on the
   data used to fit.
3. Both the **scaled and additive** forms from D-14. No silent choice.
4. Separate results for the operating rungs and for multi-rung climbs.
5. MAE **plus worst and 95th-percentile underprediction**, false-safe climbs, and
   false refusals. Symmetric MAE hides the dangerous direction.
6. Session-level confidence intervals — frames are autocorrelated and are not
   independent samples.
7. Both repeated **stationary** sweeps and ordinary moving-route sweeps.
8. A concrete bar: **at least 20% lower held-out landing MAE with no degradation
   in worst underprediction.**

## 7. The amended proposal, not yet a decision

The reviewer's alternative, recorded here and deliberately *not* written up as a
decision until someone argues for it properly:

- Use a deduplicated session P95 fit to **initialise** D-18's six ratios,
  replacing foreign-hardware seeds.
- Keep per-step live adaptation and D-20's failure memory.
- Record the fit's residual and provenance as **declared uncertainty**, not as
  truth.
- If a rung remains unobserved, permit **one explicitly bounded calibration
  probe** under a separate exploration rule.
- Test both D-14 forms.
- Address the missing-frame blind spot first.
